The Builder

The Builder

You're driven by building things from the ground up and seeing tangible results.

Builders are rare. Most people consume — you create. That instinct is the foundation of the highest-earning and most fulfilling careers in the modern economy.

Career 1 of 3

Software Engineering

The most direct path for a Builder. You write code that becomes a product millions use. Every feature you ship is something you made from nothing.

Entry Level (0–2 years)$95,000 – $130,000/yearLearn the stack, ship small features, survive code reviews.
Mid-Level (2–5 years)$130,000 – $180,000/yearOwn entire features. Lead small squads.
Senior (5–10 years)$180,000 – $280,000/yearArchitect systems. Make technical decisions that affect millions.
Staff / Principal (10+ years)$280,000 – $500,000+/yearDefine the technical direction of the company.

The path

CS degree or strong portfolio. Target: Google, Meta, Stripe, or a Series B startup for maximum learning and equity.

The insider secret

In Silicon Valley, the engineer who ships the fastest and communicates clearly wins. Learn to write. Learn to speak in meetings. Code is 60% of the job.

Career 2 of 3

Product Management

Builders who understand both technology and people become PMs — and PMs decide what gets built, why, and for whom.

Associate PM (0–2 years)$100,000 – $140,000/yearWrite specs. Talk to users. Prioritize ruthlessly.
PM (2–5 years)$140,000 – $190,000/yearOwn a product line. Work with engineering and design.
Senior PM (5–8 years)$190,000 – $260,000/yearDrive product strategy. Influence company roadmap.
VP of Product (8–15 years)$300,000 – $800,000+/yearDefine the vision. Manage a team of PMs. Massive equity.

The path

Start in engineering or design, then transition. APM programs at Google, Meta, and Microsoft are the fastest entry points.

The insider secret

The best PMs are obsessive about the user's problem — not in love with their own solution. Every week, talk to at least 3 real users.

Career 3 of 3

Technical Entrepreneurship

The highest-risk, highest-reward path for a Builder. You build the company, not just the product.

Pre-revenue (Year 0–1)$0 – ramen moneyValidate the idea. Find 10 people who pay before you build.
Early Stage (Year 1–3)Depends on fundraisingProduct-market fit. First 100 customers.
Growth Stage (Year 3–7)$200,000 – $500,000+/yearScale what works. Stop doing everything yourself.
Exit / Scale (Year 7+)$1,000,000 – unlimitedAcquisition, IPO, or profitable independence.

The path

Apply to YCombinator. Build in public on X/Twitter. Your first startup will probably fail. The second has a much higher chance.

The insider secret

Spend 50% of your first year selling, not coding. The market doesn't care how elegant your architecture is.

"The Builder's advantage is that you create value that didn't exist before. In the US economy, that is the most rewarded skill of the 21st century. The only thing standing between you and that life is years of showing up and shipping things — even when they're not perfect."

The Helper

The Helper

You're driven by healing, supporting, and elevating the human condition.

Helpers are the backbone of civilization. But the mistake most Helpers make is choosing impact over income — as if the two are mutually exclusive. They are not.

Career 1 of 3

Advanced Practice Nursing

The fastest-growing and highest-paid clinical role that doesn't require a medical degree. Nurse Practitioners diagnose, treat, and prescribe with significant autonomy.

Registered Nurse (0–3 years)$75,000 – $100,000/yearMaster clinical skills. Choose your specialty.
Nurse Practitioner (3–6 years)$110,000 – $150,000/yearDiagnose and treat independently.
Specialized NP (6–12 years)$150,000 – $220,000/yearPsychiatric, anesthesia, or acute care specializations.
Private Practice (12+ years)$200,000 – $400,000+/yearOwn your practice. Set your hours. Choose your patients.

The path

BSN (4 years) → RN license → MSN or DNP (2–3 more years). Best states: California, New York, Texas.

The insider secret

The Helpers who reach the top don't just care deeply — they learn healthcare management. One administrator makes more impact than ten individual clinicians.

Career 2 of 3

Clinical Psychology / Private Therapy

The mental health crisis in the US has created massive demand for skilled therapists. Private practice therapists choose their own hours, clients, and specializations.

Intern / Associate (0–3 years)$45,000 – $65,000/yearSupervised hours. Find your therapeutic approach.
Licensed Therapist (3–6 years)$75,000 – $110,000/yearBuild a caseload. Specialize in a niche.
Private Practice (6–12 years)$120,000 – $200,000/yearFull caseload, premium rates.
Group Practice Owner (12+ years)$200,000 – $350,000+/yearHire other therapists. Passive income.

The path

Master's in Counseling or Clinical Psychology → state licensure. Columbia, NYU, and Northwestern are top programs.

The insider secret

The therapists who earn the most build a cash-pay practice targeting high-income clients. Learning marketing is not selling out — it's how you help more people.

Career 3 of 3

Healthcare Administration

Every hospital needs leaders who understand both the human side of healthcare and how to run an organization.

Administrator (0–3 years)$60,000 – $85,000/yearOperations, scheduling, compliance.
Department Manager (3–7 years)$90,000 – $130,000/yearLead a clinical or operational department.
Director of Operations (7–12 years)$130,000 – $200,000/yearMulti-department oversight. Strategic planning.
Chief Nursing Officer (12+ years)$200,000 – $450,000+/yearC-suite. Hospital-wide decisions. Maximum impact.

The path

Undergraduate in health administration → MHA or MBA with healthcare focus. Johns Hopkins, Michigan, and Minnesota are the best programs.

The insider secret

If you want the C-suite, start taking management responsibilities within your first 5 years — not 10.

"The Helper's greatest trap is believing that wanting to earn well is selfish. It is not. A financially secure Helper helps more people, for longer, without burning out. Build your career like you mean it."

The Analyzer

The Analyzer

You're driven by pure logic, uncovering hidden patterns, and data-backed decisions.

The world runs on data, but very few people can actually think with it. Analyzers are rare — and the US economy pays a premium for rare.

Career 1 of 3

Data Science & Machine Learning

Every major company is hiring people who can turn raw data into decisions. Data Scientists are among the highest-paid professionals under 35 in the US.

Junior Data Scientist (0–2 years)$95,000 – $130,000/yearClean data, build models, present findings.
Data Scientist (2–5 years)$130,000 – $180,000/yearOwn analytical projects end-to-end.
Senior DS / ML Engineer (5–10 years)$180,000 – $280,000/yearProduction ML systems. Cross-functional influence.
Principal / Staff DS (10+ years)$280,000 – $600,000+/yearDefine data strategy. Massive equity at top companies.

The path

Strong foundation in Python, statistics, and SQL. Target: Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon, or high-growth startups for equity upside.

The insider secret

Learn to communicate like a storyteller — every analysis needs a clear "so what?" that a non-technical executive can act on in 60 seconds.

Career 2 of 3

Quantitative Finance

Wall Street pays more for mathematical precision than almost any other field. Quants build the algorithms that move billions of dollars.

Junior Quant (0–3 years)$150,000 – $250,000/yearBuild and backtest trading models.
Quant Researcher (3–7 years)$300,000 – $600,000/yearDevelop original alpha-generating strategies.
Senior Quant (7–15 years)$500,000 – $2,000,000+/yearManage capital. P&L responsibility.
Partner / Fund Manager (15+ years)$1,000,000 – $10,000,000+/yearRun strategies independently.

The path

PhD or Master's in Mathematics, Physics, or Statistics. Target firms: Two Sigma, Citadel, Jane Street, DE Shaw.

The insider secret

Jane Street tests how you think under pressure and explain your reasoning out loud. Practice competitive programming and walk through your logic clearly in real time.

Career 3 of 3

Management Consulting (Analytics Track)

McKinsey, BCG, and Bain hire Analyzers to solve the most complex business problems in the world — then pay them to travel, learn fast, and build a network that opens every door.

Business Analyst (0–2 years)$100,000 – $140,000/yearAnalyze data, build models, present to clients.
Associate (2–5 years)$150,000 – $220,000/yearLead workstreams. Client management.
Engagement Manager (5–8 years)$220,000 – $350,000/yearRun projects. Develop client relationships.
Partner (8–15 years)$500,000 – $2,000,000+/yearBring in clients. Lead the firm.

The path

Top GPA + case interview preparation. MBA from an M7 school is the most reliable path to the Associate level.

The insider secret

Consulting is a people business disguised as an analytics business. Develop your emotional intelligence as deliberately as your Excel skills.

"The Analyzer's superpower is seeing what others miss. In a world drowning in data and starving for insight, that superpower is worth more every year. The only upgrade you need is learning to make other people feel what you see."

The Leader

The Leader

You're driven by inspiring vision, driving strategy, and aligning talent toward collective victory.

Leaders are not born — they are built through repeated, high-stakes decisions made under pressure. The US economy has one rule: the person who aligns people toward a result gets paid more than anyone who executes that result.

Career 1 of 3

Management Consulting

The fastest education in business that money can buy — except they pay you. Consultants solve C-suite problems across industries, building a network no MBA alone can replicate.

Business Analyst (0–2 years)$100,000 – $140,000/yearLearn how companies work from the inside.
Associate (2–5 years)$150,000 – $230,000/yearLead client workstreams. Present to executives.
Engagement Manager (5–8 years)$230,000 – $380,000/yearOwn client relationships. Build your book of business.
Partner (8–15 years)$600,000 – $2,000,000+/yearYou are the business. Maximum autonomy.

The path

Top GPA + relentless case interview prep. McKinsey, BCG, and Bain recruit from Harvard, Wharton, Princeton, Yale, MIT, Stanford, and Dartmouth.

The insider secret

Most candidates prepare for the analytical part and neglect the leadership narrative. Partners hire people they want to put in front of their most important clients.

Career 2 of 3

Corporate Operations & General Management

Every company needs someone who can translate strategy into execution. This is the core of what Leaders do — and the path to CEO runs directly through it.

Operations Analyst (0–2 years)$70,000 – $100,000/yearProcess improvement. Cross-functional projects.
Operations Manager (2–6 years)$100,000 – $160,000/yearLead teams. Own KPIs. Drive quarterly results.
Director of Operations (6–12 years)$160,000 – $280,000/yearRun business units. Report to C-suite.
COO / CEO (12–20 years)$400,000 – several million/yearRun the company. Equity. Legacy.

The path

Business or Engineering undergraduate → 3–5 years of execution → MBA from M7 → accelerated VP track. Best training grounds: Amazon, P&G, high-growth tech.

The insider secret

The Leaders who become CEOs made the most decisions under uncertainty — and learned fastest from the ones that failed. Seek high-stakes situations early, even when you feel unready.

Career 3 of 3

Sales Leadership & Revenue

The person who brings in the revenue has more job security and earning potential than almost anyone else. Sales Leaders who build and scale teams are among the highest-paid in tech.

Account Executive (0–3 years)$80,000 – $150,000/yearClose deals. Learn the full sales cycle.
Sales Manager (3–6 years)$130,000 – $200,000/yearLead a team of AEs. Hit team quota.
VP of Sales (6–12 years)$200,000 – $400,000/yearBuild the sales org. Own revenue targets.
Chief Revenue Officer (12+ years)$400,000 – $1,500,000+/yearOwn all revenue. Report to CEO. Significant equity.

The path

No specific degree required — results are the credential. Start as an AE at a high-growth SaaS company. Salesforce and HubSpot have the best sales training in the industry.

The insider secret

The best sales leaders don't micromanage — they recruit obsessively and remove obstacles. Your job is to make your team's number, not to be the best individual seller.
